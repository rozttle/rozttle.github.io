Haiku (俳句) is a short Japanese poem that presents
the world objectively and contrasts two different images.


There are three basic rules for writing haiku:

1. 5-7-5 Structure

Traditional Japanese haiku consist of three phrases
composed of 17 morae in a 5, 7, 5 pattern

2. Seasonal Elements

Haiku have specific words you have to use.
These are called kigo (季語), or seasonal words.
For example, frog (蛙) represents spring, and moon (月つき) is normally an autumn kigo

3. A "Cutting" Word

A cutting word, called kireji (切れ字き),
has no direct equivalent in English, or most other languages.
Kireji act like punctuation in haiku that mark emphasis, juxtaposition, etc.


Each has its own important place in the anatomy of haiku.
